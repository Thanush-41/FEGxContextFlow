## Eight slide briefs and speaker notes

### 1 · Your context. In action. · 0:00–0:12

**Audience takeaway:** an instruction changes a persistent task. **Visual:** large actual app result beside the short title; use S01 only as an opening/closing surround. **Live action:** with the prepared one-selection task and warm microphone, say “Change that to five.” Hold until the exact 5.00 state is visible. **Narration after the result:** “That is the same task, continued in my own words.” **Validation:** the current captured follow-up is typed in a native voice session; the opening’s human-spoken evidence is still required. Do not label that capture a completed spoken test.

### 2 · The gap between sessions · 0:12–0:35

**Audience takeaway:** relevant research loses usefulness when the next surface forgets the task. **Visual:** three clear positions — research, app, home screen — with the same fictional event appearing only where it actually exists. Animate loss of continuity with a gap, not a fabricated error screen. **Narration:** “Research starts in one place. The action happens in another. Later, the home screen has no useful continuation. Challenge 02 asks for a reason to return. Our answer is to carry the task the user already chose, with enough context to continue it.” **Source:** FEG Challenge 02. No market-size or behavioral-causation claim.

### 3 · A task beyond the app · 0:35–1:55

**Audience takeaway:** one task survives research import, voice, review and a native surface. **Visual:** the intact 80-second native recording, with restrained S02/S03/S04 surrounds at its boundaries. The main app view occupies at least 65% of slide height; enlarge exact review and receipt details in separate deterministic overlays drawn from the same frame. **Narration:** “This is a fictional match. The research is background; the instruction is mine. I choose the outcome, change the stake, inspect the review, and confirm the exact action. The saved task then remains useful outside the app.” Pause for the actual interaction. **Evidence:** native sample-data capture; final human-spoken run pending. Never imply independent AI recommendations or a real-money placement.

### 4 · Designed to fit an operator · 1:55–2:25

**Audience takeaway:** the operator retains control over actions and eligibility. **Visual:** editable horizontal diagram: external research → versioned task → shared action handler → operator adapters / native surfaces. Show consent and eligibility checks alongside the handler, and a separate explicit-confirmation step before receipt. **Narration:** “The reusable part is the context layer. Touch, voice and handoffs use the same action handler and the same review. An operator can connect its own event catalogue, identity, exclusion and receipt systems through adapters. This build demonstrates the pattern with sample records. Production connections are a separate integration project.” **Evidence:** shared contracts, SQLite/Edge action engine and atomic receipt tests. Do not show the operator integrations as implemented.

### 5 · What success looks like · 2:25–3:00

**Audience takeaway:** useful engagement is measurable without rewarding pressure to bet. **Visual:** four large measures: useful app opens; widget adoption; surface engagement; notification opt-in retention. Small but readable companion line: task completion, off-app completion, opt-outs and harmful-play indicators. **Narration:** “We propose 1,000 participants, split equally between control and treatment, with 30 days of exposure and later retention follow-up. We measure useful opens, widget adoption, surface engagement and retained consent. A successful glance outside the app counts separately. We also watch task failures, opt-outs and harmful-play indicators. A rise in opens alone is not the success criterion.” **Evidence:** pilot proposal, not results. Existing FEG betting-session tables do not establish these baselines.

### 6 · The value and the cost · 3:00–3:55

**Audience takeaway:** the pilot must prove enough contribution to cover integration and ongoing cost. **Visual:** editable three-column adoption comparison, with the 20% case emphasized. Show 50/100/150 adopters, approximately €201/€258/€314 monthly operating cost and required contribution of €3.03/€1.58/€1.09 per adopter after assumed support savings. Separate €9,120 illustrative upfront effort. **Narration:** “At twenty percent treatment adoption, we model one hundred adopters and four hundred voice tasks a month. The illustrative operating cost is about two hundred and fifty-eight euros, including voice, transcription, infrastructure, notifications and support. After assumed support savings, the monthly break-even contribution is one euro fifty-eight per adopter. Integration and assurance add nine thousand one hundred and twenty euros in this scenario. Low adoption does not pay back. Usage, negotiated prices and identity-service quotes still need validation; this pilot is designed to resolve them.” **Evidence:** impact workbook. No precision beyond supported assumptions; no short-payback promise.

### 7 · Control stays with the user · 3:55–4:35

**Audience takeaway:** relevance requires permission and protected decisions. **Visual:** actual optional-consent screen, a visible suppression reason and exact-review detail, revealed in sequence. **Narration:** “Optional alerts start off. The demo uses device-local quiet hours from ten at night to eight in the morning, a daily cap of three, and at least an hour between alerts. These are proposed defaults. Synthetic adult, unverified, underage, at-risk and excluded records demonstrate the eligibility check. Voice cannot override them. Requested safety reminders and microphone controls remain available. A change to the slip invalidates its review.” **Evidence:** guardrail tests, real settings screenshots and native delivery code. Real register integration, accessibility audit and legal review remain production dependencies.

### 8 · The next screens · 4:35–5:00

**Audience takeaway:** a bounded pilot can test a reusable native-context layer. **Visual:** S05 Watch concept for 6 seconds; S06 Mac concept for 6 seconds; finish on the pilot ask for 13 seconds. Persistent readable “CONCEPT — not built” labels on both sequences. **Narration:** “Watch and Mac are the next concepts: the same chosen task, adapted to a smaller glance or a larger workspace. Our ask is a thirty-day FEG pilot, an integration owner and agreed measures of usefulness, cost and control. Let the user’s context become their reason to return.”
